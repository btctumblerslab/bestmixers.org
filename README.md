## Centralized mixers vs CoinJoin: which is better?

Both approaches improve Bitcoin privacy, but they work very differently.

**Centralized Bitcoin mixers** take your coins into a shared pool and send back different coins. They're easy to use, accept almost any amount and let you set custom delays and outputs. The trade-off is trust: you rely on the operator to pay out and delete logs.

**CoinJoin** builds one large joint transaction from many users' inputs. You never hand over custody of your coins, which removes the trust problem. In return you're usually limited to fixed denominations and have to wait for other participants.

Many experienced users combine both: CoinJoin for routine privacy, and a trusted centralized mixer for one-off transfers. The [rankings on BestMixers.org](https://bestmixers.org/) focus on centralized services, where choosing the right operator matters most.

## Step by step: how to mix Bitcoin safely

1. **Pick a service from a trusted ranking.** Start with the top of the list on [bestmixers.org](https://bestmixers.org/).
2. **Open it through Tor Browser.** Use the verified `.onion` link if one is available.
3. **Double-check the domain.** Compare it character by character with the verified link.
4. **Enter two or more receiving addresses.** Use fresh addresses that have never been used before.
5. **Set a random delay.** Longer delays make timing analysis much harder.
6. **Download the letter of guarantee.** Save it before you send anything.
7. **Send your BTC to the deposit address.** Send exactly once, from a single transaction.
8. **Wait for the payout.** Don't panic if it takes hours. That's the delay working.
9. **Keep the mixed coins separate.** Don't merge them with old, traceable coins.

## Warning signs of a fake Bitcoin mixer

- 🚩 The domain differs by one letter from the official one
- 🚩 No letter of guarantee, or a letter without a valid PGP signature
- 🚩 Fees far below the market average
- 🚩 Requests for an email address, phone number or ID
- 🚩 Pressure tactics like "limited-time bonus" or "send now"
- 🚩 Appears in search ads but not in independent rankings
- 🚩 No `.onion` mirror at all

If a service shows two or more of these signs, close the tab.

## FAQ: best Bitcoin mixers

### What is the best Bitcoin mixer in 2026?
The answer changes as services open, close and update their terms. The current overall winner and category leaders are always listed on [bestmixers.org](https://bestmixers.org/).

### What makes a Bitcoin mixer "the best"?
Strong privacy features, a strict no-logs policy, fair fees, a guarantee letter, Tor support and a clean track record. Price alone is never enough.

### Is it safe to use a Bitcoin mixer?
It's safer when you use a well-reviewed service through its verified link, connect over Tor and save the guarantee letter. Most losses come from phishing clones, not from established mixers.

### How do Bitcoin mixer rankings stay up to date?
Services are re-tested regularly. Offline, compromised or suspicious mixers are moved down or removed from the list.

### Do I need to create an account to mix bitcoin?
No. A trustworthy BTC mixer never asks for registration, an email address or personal documents.

### What is the minimum amount I can mix?
It varies by service, typically from about 0.001 BTC. Exact limits for each mixer are on [bestmixers.org](https://bestmixers.org/).

### Can mixed bitcoin be traced?
Good mixing with large pools, delays and multiple outputs makes tracing extremely difficult. Mistakes such as reusing addresses or merging mixed and unmixed coins can undo that protection.

### Are crypto mixers legal?
Rules differ by country. Using a mixer for personal privacy is permitted in many places, but some regulators have acted against specific services. Check your local laws first.

## Bitcoin privacy beyond mixing

A mixer is one tool, not a complete privacy strategy. For better on-chain privacy also consider:

- **Never reusing addresses.** Generate a new one for every payment.
- **Coin control.** Choose exactly which UTXOs you spend.
- **Running your own node.** Avoid leaking addresses to third-party servers.
- **Using Tor for wallet traffic.** Hide your IP from the network.
- **Separating identities.** Keep KYC coins and private coins in different wallets.

More guides on Bitcoin privacy are available at [BestMixers.org](https://bestmixers.org/).
